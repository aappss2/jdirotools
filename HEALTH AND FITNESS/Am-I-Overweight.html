Understanding Overweight: Health Risks & Tips

Being overweight means your body weight exceeds the healthy range for your height. BMI, or Body Mass Index, is commonly used to determine whether you are overweight. A BMI between 25 and 29.9 is considered overweight, while 30 and above is classified as obesity.

Overweight individuals face increased risk of health issues such as heart disease, high blood pressure, diabetes, sleep apnea, joint problems, and certain cancers. Maintaining a healthy weight is key to reducing these risks and improving overall well-being.

Causes of Being Overweight

  • Excess calorie intake compared to expenditure
  • Genetics and family history
  • Lack of physical activity
  • Hormonal imbalances
  • Poor dietary habits and high sugar/fat intake

Tips to Manage or Reduce Weight

  • Eat a balanced diet rich in vegetables, fruits, lean proteins, and whole grains
  • Reduce sugary drinks and processed foods
  • Engage in regular physical activity: cardio + strength training
  • Track calories and portion sizes
  • Consult a healthcare professional or dietitian for personalized guidance

When to Seek Medical Advice

If you are consistently overweight, experiencing rapid weight gain, or developing related health issues, consult a doctor for assessment and management options.

FAQs

Q: What BMI range is considered overweight?
A: A BMI between 25 and 29.9 is considered overweight.

Q: Can being overweight affect overall health?
A: Yes, it increases the risk of heart disease, diabetes, and other health problems.

Q: How can I safely lose weight?
A: Combine a healthy diet, portion control, and regular exercise. Avoid crash diets.

Q: Is BMI the only indicator of health?
A: No, body composition, waist-to-hip ratio, and fitness levels are also important.

Q: How often should I check my BMI?
A: Monitoring BMI every few months is sufficient, but consult your doctor for personalized advice.
