Understanding BMI (Body Mass Index)

BMI (Body Mass Index) is a widely used metric to assess whether a person has a healthy weight for their height. BMI is calculated by dividing your weight in kilograms by your height in meters squared (BMI = weight / height²).

Your BMI provides insight into whether you are underweight, normal weight, overweight, or obese. Maintaining a healthy BMI is essential for reducing the risk of chronic diseases and improving overall well-being.

BMI Categories

  • Underweight: BMI < 18.5
  • Normal weight: BMI 18.5 – 24.9
  • Overweight: BMI 25 – 29.9
  • Obese: BMI ≥ 30

Health Risks of Abnormal BMI

  • Underweight: Weak immunity, nutrient deficiencies, fragile bones
  • Overweight/Obesity: Heart disease, diabetes, hypertension, joint problems

Tips to Maintain a Healthy BMI

  • Eat a balanced diet rich in vegetables, fruits, lean proteins, and whole grains
  • Engage in regular physical activity and strength training
  • Monitor portion sizes and avoid excessive processed foods
  • Consult a healthcare professional for personalized guidance

FAQs

Q: What is a healthy BMI range?
A: 18.5 – 24.9 is considered healthy.

Q: Can BMI be inaccurate?
A: BMI does not distinguish between muscle and fat, so athletes may have higher BMI but low body fat.

Q: How often should I check my BMI?
A: Every few months is sufficient, or as advised by a healthcare professional.

Q: What should I do if my BMI is high?
A: Follow a balanced diet, exercise regularly, and consult a healthcare professional if needed.

Q: What should I do if my BMI is low?
A: Increase calorie intake with nutrient-dense foods, engage in strength training, and consult a doctor for advice.
