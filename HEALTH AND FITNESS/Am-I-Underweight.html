Understanding Underweight: Health Risks & Tips

Being underweight is when your body weight is below the healthy range for your height. It is commonly measured using the Body Mass Index (BMI), which is calculated as weight (kg) divided by height (m) squared. A BMI below 18.5 is considered underweight.

Underweight individuals may face health issues such as weakened immunity, fragile bones, fatigue, nutrient deficiencies, and potential fertility issues. Maintaining a healthy weight is essential for optimal energy, immunity, and overall well-being.

Causes of Being Underweight

  • Genetics or naturally high metabolism
  • Chronic illnesses or malabsorption
  • Excessive physical activity
  • Poor nutrition or restrictive diets
  • Stress, anxiety, or depression

Tips to Gain Healthy Weight

  • Eat nutrient-dense foods: nuts, seeds, lean protein, whole grains, and healthy fats
  • Increase calorie intake gradually
  • Eat frequent meals and snacks
  • Strength training to build muscle mass
  • Consult a nutritionist for a personalized plan

When to Seek Medical Advice

If you are consistently underweight or experiencing unexplained weight loss, consult a healthcare professional. It’s important to rule out underlying health conditions such as thyroid disorders, diabetes, or digestive issues.

FAQs

Q: What BMI range is considered underweight?
A: A BMI below 18.5 is considered underweight.

Q: Can being underweight affect fertility?
A: Yes, low body weight can impact hormone balance and fertility in both men and women.

Q: How can I gain weight safely?
A: Focus on calorie-dense, nutritious foods, strength training, and regular meals. Avoid unhealthy junk food.

Q: Is BMI the only measure of health?
A: BMI is a guideline, but body composition, muscle mass, and overall health should also be considered.

Q: How often should I check my BMI?
A: Regularly monitoring your BMI every few months is sufficient, but consult your doctor for personalized advice.
