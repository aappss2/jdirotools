Understanding Waist to Hip Ratio (WHR)

The Waist to Hip Ratio (WHR) is an important measure of body fat distribution. A high WHR indicates more abdominal fat, which is associated with higher health risks such as cardiovascular disease, diabetes, and metabolic syndrome.

How to Calculate WHR

WHR is calculated by dividing the waist circumference by the hip circumference:

WHR = Waist Circumference / Hip Circumference

WHR Health Risk Categories

  • Male: WHR < 0.90 = low risk, 0.90–0.99 = moderate risk, ≥ 1.0 = high risk
  • Female: WHR < 0.80 = low risk, 0.80–0.84 = moderate risk, ≥ 0.85 = high risk

Tips to Improve WHR

  • Maintain a healthy diet low in processed sugars and saturated fats
  • Engage in regular aerobic exercise to reduce abdominal fat
  • Include strength training to increase muscle mass
  • Monitor waist and hip measurements periodically

FAQs

Q: What is a healthy WHR?
A: Men: < 0.90, Women: < 0.80 indicate low health risk.

Q: Why is WHR important?
A: WHR indicates abdominal fat, which is linked to cardiovascular and metabolic risks.

Q: Can exercise improve WHR?
A: Yes, a combination of cardio and strength training helps reduce waist size and improve WHR.

Q: Should I track WHR regularly?
A: Yes, monthly tracking can help monitor body fat distribution changes over time.

Q: Can WHR differ from BMI?
A: Yes, WHR focuses on fat distribution, while BMI measures overall body mass.
