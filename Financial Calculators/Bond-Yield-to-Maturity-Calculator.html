Understanding Bond Yield

Bond yield is the return an investor can expect from a bond investment. It is a key metric for evaluating fixed-income securities. Current yield measures the income (coupon) relative to the bond's current market price. A higher yield can indicate higher risk or more attractive income.

Investors should consider both the current yield and yield to maturity (YTM) for a complete picture. Current yield is useful for assessing immediate income, while YTM accounts for total returns over the bond's lifetime.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is a bond's current yield?

Current yield is calculated as the annual coupon payment divided by the bond's current market price.

2. How do I calculate it?

Use the formula: Current Yield = (Annual Coupon / Market Price) × 100%. Enter your coupon and market price above.

3. Is it the same as yield to maturity?

No, yield to maturity accounts for total returns until maturity, including price changes and reinvested coupons, while current yield only looks at current income.
