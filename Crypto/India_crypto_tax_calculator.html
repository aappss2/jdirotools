Complete Guide to Cryptocurrency Tax in India (2026)

1 What is Crypto Tax in India?

Cryptocurrency tax in India refers to the taxation framework introduced by the Indian government for Virtual Digital Assets (VDAs). Since April 2022, any income earned from the transfer of cryptocurrencies, NFTs, and other digital assets is subject to a flat 30% tax under Section 115BBH of the Income Tax Act, 1961.

This landmark legislation brought clarity to the previously ambiguous tax treatment of crypto assets in India. Whether you are trading Bitcoin, Ethereum, Solana, or any other altcoin on platforms like WazirX, CoinDCX, or international exchanges, your profits are taxable under Indian law. The crypto tax India rules apply equally to all taxpayers regardless of their income slab — there is no progressive taxation for crypto income.

The Indian government treats cryptocurrency not as currency or as a traditional asset class, but as a Virtual Digital Asset (VDA). This classification has significant implications for how your gains are taxed, what deductions are allowed, and how losses are treated. Understanding these rules is crucial for every Indian crypto investor and trader to remain compliant and avoid hefty penalties.

2 How Does the 30% Flat Tax on Crypto Work?

The 30% flat tax on cryptocurrency in India is straightforward but comes with strict limitations. Here is how it works in practice:

  • Tax Rate: 30% flat on any profit earned from selling, trading, or transferring crypto assets.
  • Health & Education Cess: An additional 4% cess is applied on the tax amount, making the effective rate approximately 31.2%.
  • Surcharge: If your total income exceeds certain thresholds, a surcharge may also apply, increasing the effective rate further.
  • Deduction Allowed: Only the cost of acquisition (purchase price) can be deducted from the sale price to calculate profit. No other expenses like trading fees, internet costs, or advisory fees are deductible.
  • No Loss Set-Off: Losses from one cryptocurrency cannot be set off against gains from another cryptocurrency or any other income source.

For example, if you bought Bitcoin for ₹1,00,000 and sold it for ₹1,50,000, your profit is ₹50,000. The tax would be ₹50,000 × 30% = ₹15,000, and the cess would be ₹15,000 × 4% = ₹600. Your total tax payable would be ₹15,600. This cryptocurrency tax calculator India tool above automates this calculation for you instantly.

3 What is the 1% TDS on Crypto Transactions?

Under Section 194S of the Income Tax Act, a 1% Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) is applicable on every crypto transaction. This TDS is deducted at the time of the transaction by either the buyer or the exchange platform.

The 1% TDS threshold is ₹10,000 per financial year for regular individuals and ₹50,000 per financial year for specified persons (those whose income is being audited). Once your total crypto transaction value crosses this threshold, TDS is applicable on the entire amount.

It is important to understand that TDS is not an additional tax — it is a tax collected in advance. The TDS amount can be adjusted against your final tax liability when you file your Income Tax Return (ITR). If excess TDS has been deducted, you can claim a refund. Most Indian crypto exchanges like WazirX, CoinDCX, and CoinSwitch automatically deduct this 1% TDS from your transactions.

4 How to Calculate Crypto Tax — Step by Step

Calculating your crypto tax in India is a systematic process. Follow these steps to determine your tax liability accurately:

  1. Determine Sale Price: Record the total amount received from selling or transferring your crypto assets in Indian Rupees (₹).
  2. Determine Cost of Acquisition: Calculate the total purchase price of the crypto assets you sold. This is the only deduction allowed.
  3. Calculate Net Profit: Subtract the cost of acquisition from the sale price. If the result is positive, you have taxable profit. If negative, you have a loss (which cannot be set off).
  4. Apply 30% Tax: Multiply your net profit by 30% to get the base tax amount.
  5. Add 4% Cess: Calculate 4% of the tax amount and add it. This is the Health and Education Cess.
  6. Check for Surcharge: If your total income exceeds ₹50 lakhs, additional surcharge may apply.
  7. Deduct TDS Already Paid: Subtract any 1% TDS already deducted by exchanges from your total liability.

5 Capital Gains and Crypto — India Does NOT Differentiate

One of the most common misconceptions among Indian crypto investors is that holding crypto for a longer period will result in lower taxes, similar to equity investments. This is incorrect.

Unlike stocks and mutual funds where short-term capital gains (STCG) and long-term capital gains (LTCG) are taxed at different rates, India does not differentiate between short-term and long-term gains for cryptocurrency. Whether you hold your Bitcoin for 1 day or 5 years, the tax rate remains a flat 30% plus 4% cess under Section 115BBH.

This is a critical distinction that sets crypto taxation apart from traditional asset classes in India. The rationale behind this is that the government classifies crypto as a Virtual Digital Asset (VDA) rather than a capital asset, placing it outside the purview of regular capital gains provisions under Sections 111A and 112A of the Income Tax Act. Therefore, concepts like indexation benefits or reduced rates for long-term holding do not apply to cryptocurrency gains in India.

6 Important Compliance Tips for Crypto Traders

Staying compliant with India's crypto tax regulations is essential to avoid penalties and legal issues. Here are key compliance tips every crypto trader and investor should follow:

  • Maintain Detailed Records: Keep a comprehensive record of all your crypto transactions including dates, amounts, exchange used, and transaction IDs. This documentation is critical during ITR filing and potential scrutiny.
  • Report All Transactions: Every crypto transaction must be reported in your ITR, even if you incurred a loss. Non-reporting can attract penalties under Section 270A.
  • Use the Correct ITR Form: Crypto income should be reported in the appropriate ITR form. Salaried individuals with crypto income may need to use ITR-2 or ITR-3 instead of ITR-1.
  • Pay Advance Tax: If your estimated crypto tax liability exceeds ₹10,000 in a financial year, you must pay advance tax in quarterly installments.
  • Verify TDS Credits: Check your Form 26AS and AIS (Annual Information Statement) to ensure all TDS deducted on your crypto transactions is correctly reflected.
  • Report Crypto Holdings: If you hold foreign crypto assets or accounts on international exchanges, you may need to disclose them under the Foreign Asset schedule in your ITR.
  • Consult a CA: Given the evolving nature of crypto regulations in India, always consult a qualified Chartered Accountant for personalized advice.

7 Understanding Crypto Gifts and Airdrops Tax

If you receive cryptocurrency as a gift or through airdrops, it is also taxable in India. Crypto gifts exceeding ₹50,000 in value during a financial year are treated as "Income from Other Sources" and taxed at your applicable slab rate. When you eventually sell the gifted crypto, the 30% flat tax under Section 115BBH applies to the profit (sale price minus the cost to the previous owner or fair market value on the date of the gift, whichever is lower).

Airdrops are similarly taxable at the time of receipt based on their fair market value. Mining income is also subject to taxation. The cost of acquisition for mined crypto is considered nil, meaning the entire sale proceeds would be taxable at 30%.
