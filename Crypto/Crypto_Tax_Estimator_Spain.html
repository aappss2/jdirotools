    How Cryptocurrency Is Taxed in Spain (2026 Guide)

    Spain has become one of the most active crypto markets in the EU, and the Spanish tax authority, Agencia Tributaria, expects transparent reporting. If you buy, sell, exchange, or move cryptocurrency, you are likely creating a taxable event. This guide focuses on crypto tax Spain rules for 2026, helping investors understand how the savings tax brackets apply, how losses can be used, and which reporting forms are required. Use the calculator above to estimate your liability before you complete your annual return.

    Capital Gains Tax on Crypto Assets

    Most crypto transactions are treated as capital gains or losses. A gain occurs when the sale price of a cryptocurrency exceeds the acquisition cost plus allowable fees and expenses. This is true whether you sell crypto for euros, swap it for another token, or use crypto to pay for goods and services. When you dispose of assets, you calculate a net gain or loss, and the result is taxed under the savings income rules. This is the same category used for shares, ETFs, and other financial assets, which is why the term cryptocurrency tax calculator Spain is often linked to the savings base. Note that gifting crypto or transferring between wallets can have different consequences, so you should track each transaction carefully.

    Spanish Savings Tax Brackets Explained

    For 2026, Spain applies progressive brackets to the savings tax base. The first €6,000 of gains is taxed at 19%, then 21% applies between €6,001 and €50,000. From €50,001 to €200,000, the rate increases to 23%. The next band between €200,001 and €300,000 is 27%, and anything above €300,000 is taxed at 28%. This structure encourages accurate reporting because the effective tax rate rises as gains increase. If you are searching for bitcoin tax Spain or crypto capital gains Spain 2026, these rates are the key reference point for your tax bill.

    Agencia Tributaria Reporting Requirements

    Agencia Tributaria treats crypto like other financial assets, but it expects detailed records. The main annual return for residents is the Modelo 100 (IRPF). You must include capital gains or losses in the savings tax base and retain evidence of transactions such as exchange statements, wallet records, and fee receipts. If you operate on multiple platforms, keep a consolidated ledger for your trading history. Many taxpayers also need to consider whether they have foreign holdings that trigger separate reporting duties. If you have holdings abroad above the threshold, the new Modelo 721 is relevant.

    Modelo 100: Annual Income Tax Return

    The Modelo 100 is the standard annual personal income tax return. Crypto gains are included in the savings base, and you must report the net outcome after including fees and other allowable costs. For active traders, this often means dozens or hundreds of transactions, which is why robust record keeping is essential. The tax authority can request supporting documents even years later, so you should keep records for at least four years after filing.

    Modelo 721: Foreign Crypto Reporting

    Modelo 721 is focused on crypto assets held abroad, such as balances on non-Spanish exchanges or foreign custodial wallets. The reporting obligation is separate from your annual tax return and is designed to provide transparency on offshore holdings. It is a high-priority compliance issue for the Spanish government. If you are unsure whether your wallet is considered foreign or domestic, consult a professional, because failure to report can lead to penalties. This is a core topic for anyone researching impuesto criptomonedas España for 2026.

    Loss Compensation Rules and Offsets

    Crypto losses can be used to offset gains in the same tax year. If losses exceed gains, they can offset other savings income up to 25% of that category. Any remaining losses can be carried forward to future years. This makes it critical to report losses accurately, even if you did not realize a net gain overall. In practice, a loss can reduce your tax bill significantly, and the calculator above will show when your net result is negative. If you experience volatility, remember that proper timing of sales can impact the effective tax rate.

    Wealth Tax Implications

    Spain applies a wealth tax in some regions, and crypto holdings may be included in the taxable base. The thresholds and rates vary by autonomous community, so the impact can differ between Madrid, Catalonia, Valencia, and other regions. The calculator on this page focuses only on capital gains tax and does not include wealth tax liabilities. If your portfolio is significant, consider regional thresholds and potential exemptions when planning your year-end strategy.

    Holding Versus Selling: Tax Impact

    Holding crypto does not generally create a taxable event, but selling, swapping, or using crypto for purchases does. This means that a buy-and-hold strategy may defer tax until you dispose of the asset. However, if you use crypto for everyday spending, each transaction can create a gain or loss. This often surprises investors and is a major reason why accurate cost-basis tracking is essential. The question “Do I pay tax if I just hold crypto?” is common, and the short answer is no, but you may still have reporting obligations for foreign holdings under Modelo 721.

    Compliance Tips for 2026

    To stay compliant, keep a clear list of transaction dates, amounts, euro values at the time of each trade, and associated fees. Use a consistent method for calculating cost basis. Keep screenshots or CSV exports from exchanges, and store wallet transaction hashes. When in doubt, err on the side of transparency. Good documentation not only reduces stress but also builds credibility if Agencia Tributaria requests verification. This is essential for any taxpayer wanting to avoid penalties and optimize their tax position.

    Record Keeping and Documentation

    Record keeping is the foundation of a strong tax position. If you move crypto between wallets or exchanges, document those transfers so they are not mistaken for disposals. If you receive airdrops, staking rewards, or other income, evaluate whether they are taxed as income or capital gains and note the fair market value at receipt. Accurate records allow you to reconcile trades, calculate exact net gains, and defend your filings. Many taxpayers face challenges when they lack complete records for multiple years, which is why building a consistent routine is recommended.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Is crypto legal in Spain?

    Yes, cryptocurrency is legal in Spain. The Spanish tax authority regulates reporting and taxation, but ownership and trading are permitted.

    How much tax do I pay on Bitcoin in Spain?

    Bitcoin gains are taxed under the savings income brackets, ranging from 19% to 28% for 2026 depending on your total gains.

    Do I need to declare crypto holdings?

    You must declare capital gains or losses in Modelo 100. If you hold crypto abroad above thresholds, Modelo 721 may apply.

    What is Modelo 721?

    Modelo 721 is the declaration for crypto assets held abroad and is separate from the annual income tax return.

    Can I offset crypto losses?

    Yes. Losses can offset capital gains and up to 25% of savings income, with remaining losses carried forward.

    Do I pay tax if I just hold crypto?

    Holding crypto generally does not trigger tax, but reporting obligations like Modelo 721 can still apply for foreign holdings.
