Complete Guide to Cryptocurrency Taxation in Japan (2026)

Understanding how cryptocurrency is taxed in Japan is essential for every crypto investor, trader, and enthusiast. The Japanese tax system treats virtual currencies (仮想通貨) as a unique asset class with specific rules that differ significantly from many other countries. This comprehensive guide covers everything you need to know about crypto taxation under National Tax Agency (NTA) regulations.

How Cryptocurrency is Taxed in Japan

In Japan, cryptocurrency is not treated as currency but as a type of property. When you sell, trade, or use crypto, any profit you make is subject to taxation. The National Tax Agency (国税庁) has established clear guidelines on how these transactions should be reported and taxed.

Unlike some countries that treat crypto as capital gains, Japan classifies cryptocurrency profits as miscellaneous income (雑所得). This classification has significant implications for how your crypto gains are calculated and what deductions may be available.

Classification as Miscellaneous Income (雑所得)

Miscellaneous income is one of ten income categories under Japanese tax law. It covers income that doesn't fall into other specific categories like employment income, business income, or real estate income. Cryptocurrency gains fall into this category because they don't fit the traditional definitions of other income types.

The key characteristics of miscellaneous income include:

  • Progressive tax rates apply (5% to 45%)
  • Losses generally cannot offset other income types
  • No special deductions available
  • Must be reported on annual tax returns

Progressive Tax System Explanation

Japan uses a progressive tax system where higher income levels are taxed at higher rates. For cryptocurrency gains classified as miscellaneous income, the following national income tax brackets apply:

National vs Resident Tax

In addition to national income tax, cryptocurrency gains are subject to resident tax (住民税). This local tax is levied by prefectures and municipalities where you reside. The standard resident tax rate is 10%, consisting of:

  • Prefectural tax: 4%
  • Municipal tax: 6%

Resident tax is calculated based on your previous year's income and is typically paid in four installments throughout the year. When combined with national income tax, the total tax burden on crypto gains can range from 15% to 55% depending on your income level.

Reconstruction Special Income Tax (2.1%)

The Reconstruction Special Income Tax (復興特別所得税) was introduced to fund recovery efforts following the 2011 Tohoku earthquake and tsunami. This additional tax applies to national income tax at a rate of 2.1%.

For example, if your national income tax on crypto gains is 100,000 JPY, you would pay an additional 2,100 JPY in reconstruction tax. While this may seem small, it's an important component of your total tax liability.

Mining Taxation

Cryptocurrency mining income is taxable in Japan. When you successfully mine cryptocurrency, the fair market value of the coins at the time of mining is considered miscellaneous income. This applies whether you're mining Bitcoin, Ethereum, or other cryptocurrencies.

Additionally, when you later sell or exchange the mined cryptocurrency, any appreciation in value is also subject to tax. This creates a potential double taxation scenario where mining rewards are taxed when received and again when sold at a profit.

If mining constitutes a business activity, you may be able to deduct related expenses such as electricity costs, hardware depreciation, and maintenance fees. However, hobby miners typically cannot claim these deductions.

Staking Taxation

Staking rewards are treated similarly to mining income. When you receive staking rewards, they're taxed as miscellaneous income based on the fair market value at the time of receipt. This applies to Proof-of-Stake cryptocurrencies like Ethereum 2.0, Cardano, and Polkadot.

The NTA has clarified that staking rewards are taxable even if you don't sell them immediately. This means you may owe tax on rewards even if you haven't converted them to fiat currency.

NFT Taxation Overview

Non-fungible tokens (NFTs) are also subject to Japanese tax rules. Whether you're creating, buying, or selling NFTs, various tax implications apply:

  • Creating and selling NFTs: Income may be classified as business income if done professionally, or miscellaneous income if occasional
  • Buying and selling NFTs: Profits are generally treated as miscellaneous income
  • NFT trading: Gains from trading NFTs are taxable events

Loss Treatment Limitations

One of the most significant limitations in Japanese crypto taxation is the treatment of losses. Unlike some countries where capital losses can offset other income, Japan's classification of crypto as miscellaneous income means:

  • Crypto losses can only offset gains from the same miscellaneous income category
  • Losses cannot offset salary or employment income
  • Excess losses cannot be carried forward to future years
  • Losses from different types of miscellaneous income generally cannot be combined

This limitation makes tax planning particularly important for crypto investors in Japan.

Record Keeping Requirements

Proper record keeping is essential for crypto tax compliance. The NTA requires taxpayers to maintain detailed records of all cryptocurrency transactions, including:

  • Transaction dates
  • Type of transaction (buy, sell, trade, mining, staking)
  • Amount of cryptocurrency involved
  • JPY value at the time of transaction
  • Fees paid
  • Counterparty information (exchange or wallet)

Records should be kept for at least seven years. Many taxpayers use specialized crypto tax software or spreadsheets to track their transactions.

National Tax Agency (NTA) Reporting

Cryptocurrency gains must be reported on your annual tax return (確定申告). The filing period is typically from February 16 to March 15 of the following year. If your miscellaneous income from crypto exceeds 200,000 JPY, you're required to file a tax return.

The NTA has been increasingly vigilant about crypto tax compliance. They receive transaction data from Japanese exchanges and are developing systems to track international transactions as well.

Compliance Risks

Failure to properly report cryptocurrency gains can result in serious consequences:

  • Additional taxes (up to 40% for intentional non-reporting)
  • Late payment interest
  • Penalties for underreporting
  • Criminal charges for tax evasion in severe cases
  • Damage to immigration or visa status for foreign residents

The NTA has indicated that cryptocurrency tax compliance is a priority, and they're using advanced analytics to identify non-compliant taxpayers.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is Bitcoin taxed in Japan?

Bitcoin and other cryptocurrencies are treated as miscellaneous income (雑所得) in Japan. Profits from selling, trading, or using crypto are subject to progressive income tax rates ranging from 5% to 45%, plus 10% resident tax. The tax is calculated on your total annual gains.

What is Miscellaneous Income?

Miscellaneous income (雑所得) is a Japanese tax category for income that doesn't fall into other specific categories like employment income or business income. Cryptocurrency gains are classified as miscellaneous income, which means they're taxed at progressive rates but losses generally cannot offset other types of income.

Do I pay resident tax on crypto?

Yes, in addition to national income tax, you must pay resident tax (住民税) on cryptocurrency gains. The standard resident tax rate is 10% (4% prefectural tax + 6% municipal tax), applied to your total taxable income including crypto gains.

Can crypto losses offset salary income?

No, under current Japanese tax law, cryptocurrency losses cannot offset salary or other employment income. Crypto is classified as miscellaneous income, and losses from miscellaneous income can only offset gains from the same category. This is a significant limitation compared to some other countries.

Is staking taxable?

Yes, staking rewards are taxable in Japan. When you receive staking rewards, they're treated as miscellaneous income based on the fair market value at the time of receipt. Additionally, when you later sell or dispose of those rewards, any appreciation is also subject to capital gains tax.

What tax rate applies to crypto?

Cryptocurrency gains are subject to Japan's progressive income tax rates: 5%, 10%, 20%, 23%, 33%, 40%, or 45% depending on your total taxable income. Additionally, you'll pay 10% resident tax and potentially 2.1% reconstruction special income tax. The combined rate can reach up to 55% for high-income earners.

Do I need to file a tax return?

You must file a tax return if your miscellaneous income from crypto exceeds 200,000 JPY annually, or if your total income (including salary) requires filing. Even if below the threshold, filing may be beneficial for claiming deductions or if you have other income sources.

What happens if I don't declare crypto gains?

Failure to declare cryptocurrency gains can result in penalties including additional taxes (up to 40% for intentional non-reporting), late payment interest, and potential criminal charges for tax evasion. The NTA is increasingly monitoring crypto transactions through exchange reporting requirements.
