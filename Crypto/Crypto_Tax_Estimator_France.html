Complete Guide to Cryptocurrency Taxation in France (2026)

Understanding how cryptocurrency is taxed in France is essential for every digital asset holder. The French tax system has evolved significantly to accommodate the growing crypto economy, establishing clear rules under Article 150 VH bis of the Code Général des Impôts (CGI). This comprehensive guide explains everything you need to know about crypto tax france regulations, including the PFU flat tax, reporting obligations, and compliance requirements for 2026.

How Cryptocurrency Is Taxed in France

France treats cryptocurrency as movable property (biens meubles) rather than currency. This classification means that capital gains from bitcoin tax france transactions are subject to specific tax rules distinct from ordinary income. The taxation occurs only when you convert cryptocurrency back to fiat currency (euros), not when trading between different cryptocurrencies.

The french crypto capital gains tax 2026 framework applies the global portfolio method for calculating gains. This means you calculate your overall position across all crypto assets rather than tracking individual coin purchases and sales. When you sell any portion of your portfolio, the gain is determined based on the ratio of your total portfolio value to your total acquisition cost.

PFU (Prélèvement Forfaitaire Unique) Explained

The pfu crypto tax is France's flat tax system designed to simplify cryptocurrency taxation. Introduced to provide clarity and predictability, the PFU applies a single flat rate of 30% to net capital gains from digital asset sales. This rate breaks down into two components: 12.8% income tax and 17.2% social contributions.

The 12.8% income tax portion represents the prélevé à la source, while the 17.2% social contributions include CSG (Contribution Sociale Généralisée), CRDS (Contribution pour le Remboursement de la Dette Sociale), and other social levies. This combined 30% rate applies automatically unless you elect for the progressive income tax option.

When Progressive Tax Option Applies

While the PFU flat tax is the default option, French taxpayers may choose to apply progressive income tax rates instead. This option becomes advantageous when your marginal tax rate is below 30%. French income tax brackets for 2026 range from 0% to 45%, with brackets at 11%, 30%, 41%, and 45%.

If your total taxable income places you in the 11% bracket, electing progressive taxation could significantly reduce your crypto tax liability. However, remember that social contributions of 17.2% still apply regardless of which income tax option you choose. The election for progressive taxation must be made when filing your annual tax return and applies to all capital gains for that year.

Occasional Trader vs Professional Trader (BIC Regime)

French tax law distinguishes between occasional traders and professional traders. Occasional traders benefit from the PFU flat tax or progressive income tax options described above. Professional traders, however, fall under the BIC (Bénéfices Industriels et Commerciaux) regime, which treats crypto trading as commercial activity.

Several factors determine professional trader status: frequency of transactions, use of specialized tools or algorithms, proportion of income derived from trading, and whether trading constitutes your primary activity. Professional traders must register as self-employed (auto-entrepreneur or entreprise individuelle) and are subject to different accounting and tax rules, including potential VAT obligations.

How Gains Are Calculated (Global Portfolio Method)

The global portfolio method (méthode du portefeuille global) simplifies gain calculation by considering your entire crypto holdings as a single pool. Here's how it works: First, calculate your total acquisition cost (prix total d'acquisition) by summing all amounts spent to purchase cryptocurrencies. Then determine your total portfolio value at the time of sale.

When you sell a portion of your holdings, the taxable gain is calculated using the formula: (Sale Price - Total Acquisition Cost × Sale Value / Total Portfolio Value). This proportional method ensures fair taxation regardless of which specific coins you sell. Trading fees can be deducted from your gains, reducing your overall tax liability.

Reporting Obligations (Form 2086 and 2042-C)

French residents must report cryptocurrency transactions using Form 2086, the dedicated digital asset declaration form. This form requires detailed information about each taxable sale, including the date of acquisition, date of sale, sale price, acquisition cost, and calculated gain or loss. Form 2086 must be filed electronically through your personal tax account (impots.gouv.fr).

The net result from Form 2086 transfers to Form 2042-C (Cerfa 2042 Complémentaire), which is part of your annual income tax return. Even if you have no taxable gain, you must file these forms to declare your crypto activity. Failure to report can result in penalties of up to 150% of the tax owed plus potential criminal sanctions for tax fraud.

Foreign Exchange Reporting Requirement

If you hold cryptocurrency accounts with foreign exchanges or platforms, you must declare these accounts using Form 3916 (Déclaration d'ouverture de compte à l'étranger). This requirement applies to accounts held with exchanges based outside France, including popular platforms like Binance, Coinbase, Kraken, and others.

Form 3916 must be filed annually for each foreign account, even if you had no transactions during the year. The deadline aligns with your regular tax return. Non-compliance can result in penalties of €1,500 per undeclared account (€10,000 for accounts in non-cooperative jurisdictions). Keep records of all your exchange accounts and their jurisdictions to ensure complete compliance.

Loss Treatment Rules

Cryptocurrency capital losses are fully deductible against gains in the same tax year under the global portfolio method. If your total sales result in a net loss, this loss can offset other capital gains from digital assets. Losses exceeding gains may be carried forward to future years, though specific rules apply to the carryforward period.

Proper documentation is essential for claiming loss deductions. Maintain records of all transactions, including dates, amounts, fees, and exchange rates. Losses from crypto-to-crypto exchanges are not immediately deductible since these are non-taxable events. Only losses from sales converting crypto to fiat currency can be recognized for tax purposes.

Record Keeping Requirements

French tax authorities require comprehensive record keeping for all cryptocurrency transactions. You must maintain documentation supporting your acquisition costs, sale prices, trading fees, and exchange rates for at least five years. Acceptable records include exchange statements, bank transfer receipts, wallet addresses, and blockchain transaction IDs.

Consider using specialized crypto tax software to track your transactions automatically. These tools can generate Form 2086-compatible reports and help ensure accuracy. Even with software, retain original exchange records as backup documentation. The burden of proof lies with the taxpayer, so thorough record keeping protects you in case of a tax audit.

Legal Compliance Tips

To ensure full compliance with french crypto tax regulations, follow these best practices: First, report all taxable sales accurately and on time. Second, declare all foreign exchange accounts using Form 3916. Third, maintain detailed transaction records for at least five years. Fourth, consider consulting a qualified French tax adviser for complex situations.

Stay informed about regulatory changes, as crypto taxation rules continue to evolve. The EU's Markets in Crypto-Assets (MiCA) regulation introduces new compliance requirements that may affect French taxpayers. Subscribe to official updates from the Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) and consult professional resources regularly.
