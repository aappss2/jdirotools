PNG Color Replacer: Replace Any Color in Your Image

Click to sample a color, adjust tolerance and strength, optionally preserve luminance for natural shading, and stack multiple rules. Everything runs locally in your browser.

Why use a color replacer?

Quickly swap brand colors, recolor UI elements, change artwork accents, or standardize assets without round‑tripping through heavy editors. Perfect for designers, marketers, and devs who need precise, consistent results fast.

How it works

  1. Upload an image (PNG/JPG/WebP) or load the sample.
  2. Add a rule: pick a Target color and a Replace color.
  3. Adjust Tolerance (what counts as “similar”) and Strength (how far to push the color).
  4. Choose a Color distance mode (RGB/HSV/YCbCr).
  5. Enable Preserve luminance to keep highlights/shadows natural.
  6. Stack multiple rules for multi-color replacements, then download PNG.

Color distance modes explained

  • RGB: direct channel distance; simple but sensitive to brightness.
  • HSV: focuses on hue/saturation; good for hue‑centric swaps.
  • YCbCr: separates luminance (Y) from chroma (Cb/Cr); often most natural for photos.

Tips for best results

  • Use Preserve luminance for realistic shading and depth.
  • Start with moderate Tolerance (30–60) and fine‑tune.
  • Increase Softness for smoother transitions on gradients.
  • Turn on Mask overlay to visualize exactly what will change.

Common use cases

  • Swap brand colors across icons and UI screenshots
  • Change shirt/product colors in lifestyle images (subtle edits)
  • Unify chart colors or infographic accents
  • Recolor illustrations while keeping shading

Privacy & performance

Processing happens locally in your browser — no uploads, no watermarks, no waiting. The tool uses high‑quality canvas rendering for fast previews and a PNG export.

FAQ: PNG Color Replacer

What does Tolerance do?

Tolerance controls how similar a pixel must be to the target color to be affected. Higher values affect a wider range of similar colors.

What is Preserve luminance?

It keeps the original light/dark values (luminance) and only swaps hue/saturation, so shading remains natural instead of flat.

Which color distance should I use?

YCbCr often looks most natural for photos. HSV is useful for hue‑focused changes. RGB is simple and OK for flat graphics.

Can I replace multiple colors at once?

Yes. Add multiple rules. They run in order and each rule updates the image before the next one applies.

What does “Only opaque” mean?

If enabled, very transparent pixels are ignored based on the Opaque threshold. Useful for icons or assets with antialiased edges.

Why won’t download work for some images?

If you drag an image from another site, the browser may block canvas export (cross‑origin). Use a local file or the built‑in sample.

Is this free and private?

Yes. It runs entirely in your browser, free and watermark‑free.
