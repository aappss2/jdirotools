📖 Core Definitions

Active Voice

Definition: The subject performs the action directly

Formula: Subject + Verb + Object
Example: Ravi writes letters.

Passive Voice

Definition: The subject receives the action; focus shifts to what happens to the subject

Formula: Object (becomes subject) + form of be + Past Participle + (by + original subject)
Example: Letters are written by Ravi.

⭐ The Golden Rule for Voice Conversion

1 Find the object in active sentence
2 Make it the new subject
3 Use correct be form + Past Participle (V3)
4 Add by + original subject (if needed)
5 Adjust pronouns and verb agreement

💡 Remember: This 5-step process works for ALL conversions!

💡 Let's See It In Action

→

🔍 How to Identify Active vs. Passive Voice

Quick Identification Method:

Step 1: Look for forms of "be" + Past Participle (V3)
am/is/are + V3, was/were + V3, will be + V3, etc.
Step 2: Check if the subject is doing or receiving the action
Step 3: Look for "by" + agent (though not always present)

Quick Practice:

Identify: "The song was sung beautifully."

✅ Passive - "was sung" (be + V3), subject "song" receives the action

🔍 Practice Identification

"The children are playing in the park."

Active - Subject "children" is performing the action "playing"

"The homework was completed by students."

Passive - "was completed" (be + V3), subject "homework" receives action

"Someone has stolen my bicycle."

Active - Subject "someone" is performing the action "has stolen"

"The problem is being solved."

Passive - "is being solved" (be + being + V3)

🎯 Key Points for Perfect Identification

Signs of Active Voice

  • Subject clearly does the action
  • Direct object receives the action
  • No "be + V3" pattern
  • Action flows from subject to object

Signs of Passive Voice

  • Forms of "be" + Past Participle
  • Subject receives the action
  • Often has "by + agent" (but not always)
  • Focus on what happens to subject
📝 Exam Tip: If you see "by" in the sentence, it's usually passive. But passive can exist without "by"!

📊 Simple Tenses Conversion Chart

Tense Active Formula Passive Formula Example
Simple Present S + V1 + O O + am/is/are + V3 + by S
She teaches math
Math is taught by her
Simple Past S + V2 + O O + was/were + V3 + by S
He wrote a book
A book was written by him
Simple Future S + will + V1 + O O + will be + V3 + by S
They will finish work
Work will be finished by them

💡 Pattern: Notice how the "be" form changes with tense, but V3 remains constant!

📊 Continuous Tenses Conversion

Tense Active Formula Passive Formula Example
Present Continuous S + am/is/are + V-ing + O O + am/is/are being + V3 + by S
I am reading a story
A story is being read by me
Past Continuous S + was/were + V-ing + O O + was/were being + V3 + by S
She was cooking dinner
Dinner was being cooked by her
⚠️ Important: Future Continuous and Perfect Continuous tenses are rarely used in passive voice!

📊 Perfect Tenses Conversion

Tense Active Formula Passive Formula Example
Present Perfect S + has/have + V3 + O O + has/have been + V3 + by S
We have completed the task
The task has been completed by us
Past Perfect S + had + V3 + O O + had been + V3 + by S
He had finished homework
Homework had been finished by him
Future Perfect S + will have + V3 + O O + will have been + V3 + by S
They will have built the house
The house will have been built by them

🔄 Modal Verbs in Passive Voice

Formula: Modal + be + V3

Active: S + Modal + V1 + O

Passive: O + Modal + be + V3 + by S

He can drive a car
→
A car can be driven by him
You should respect elders
→
Elders should be respected by you
We must follow rules
→
Rules must be followed by us
She might win the race
→
The race might be won by her

❗ Imperative Sentences in Passive

Formula: Let + Object + be + V3

Close the door
→
Let the door be closed
Please help me
→
Let me be helped, please
Don't touch this
→
Let this not be touched
Study regularly
→
You are advised to study regularly
💡 Alternative: Sometimes we use "You are requested/advised to..." for polite imperatives

❓ Questions in Passive Voice

Wh-word + be + Subject + V3 + by + Object?

Who broke the window?
→
By whom was the window broken?
What did you write?
→
What was written by you?
Did she complete the work?
→
Was the work completed by her?
⚠️ Remember: "Who" becomes "by whom" in passive questions!

🔄 Ditransitive Verbs (Two Objects)

When a sentence has TWO objects, we can make EITHER object the subject in passive voice.

Active: The teacher gave the students books.
Passive 1: The students were given books by the teacher. (Indirect object becomes subject)
Passive 2: Books were given to the students by the teacher. (Direct object becomes subject)

More Examples:

She told me a story
→
I was told a story by her
He sent her a letter
→
She was sent a letter by him

🔗 Phrasal & Prepositional Verbs

Rule: Keep the preposition/particle with the verb!

They looked after the children
→
The children were looked after by them
He switched on the light
→
The light was switched on by him
They called off the meeting
→
The meeting was called off by them
She laughed at him
→
He was laughed at by her
💡 Key Point: Don't separate the phrasal verb - "looked after", "switched on", etc. stay together!

⚡ Special Cases & Tricky Situations

1. Intransitive Verbs

Cannot be made passive (no object to become subject)

✅ The accident happened
❌ The accident was happened

2. Linking Verbs

Be, seem, appear, become - generally not used in passive

✅ She is intelligent
❌ Intelligent is been by her

3. When "by" is not needed

When the agent is unknown, unimportant, or obvious

English is spoken worldwide
My bike was stolen
The bridge is being repaired

🎯 Advanced Passive Constructions

1. Complex Sentences

I know that he completed the work
→
It is known to me that the work was completed by him

2. Infinitive Constructions

They expect him to win
→
He is expected to win

3. Causative Constructions

I got him to repair my car
→
I got my car repaired
📝 Advanced Tip: These constructions appear in higher-level exams!

✍️ When to Use Active vs. Passive

Use Active When:

  • The doer is important or known
  • You want direct, clear communication
  • Writing instructions or procedures
  • Expressing personal opinions
  • Making the writing more engaging
Example: "The doctor treated the patient carefully."

Use Passive When:

  • The doer is unknown or unimportant
  • You want to emphasize the action/result
  • Writing scientific or formal reports
  • Being diplomatic or indirect
  • The subject is obvious from context
Example: "Mistakes were made in the report."

🚫 Top 10 Common Errors to Avoid

1
❌ The book were read by me
✅ The book was read by me
Wrong be form - singular subject needs "was"
2
❌ The song was sang beautifully
✅ The song was sung beautifully
Wrong past participle - "sang" is past tense, "sung" is V3
3
❌ The work must finished soon
✅ The work must be finished soon
Missing "be" with modal
4
❌ The accident was happened
✅ The accident happened
Intransitive verb cannot be passive
5
❌ By who was this written?
✅ By whom was this written?
Use "whom" for object form

✅ Voice Conversion Checklist

Before Converting:

  • □ Identify the current voice (Active or Passive)
  • □ Locate the subject, verb, and object
  • □ Determine the tense
  • □ Check if conversion is possible (transitive verb?)

During Conversion:

  • □ Swap subject and object positions
  • □ Use correct form of "be" + V3
  • □ Add "by" + agent (if needed)
  • □ Maintain the same tense
  • □ Adjust pronouns (I↔me, he↔him, etc.)

After Converting:

  • □ Check subject-verb agreement
  • □ Verify correct past participle
  • □ Ensure sentence makes sense
  • □ Confirm same meaning as original

📚 BlackBoard Summary

Key Formulas:

Active: Subject + Verb + Object
Passive: Object + be form + V3 + (by Subject)

  • Only transitive verbs can be passive
  • Tense remains the same in conversion
  • "by" is optional if agent is unimportant
