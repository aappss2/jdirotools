Understanding Economy Rate in Cricket

Economy rate is one of the most important statistics for a bowler in limited-overs cricket. It measures how many runs a bowler concedes per over bowled. The formula is:

Economy Rate = Runs Conceded ÷ Overs Bowled

For example, if a bowler concedes 40 runs in 8 overs, their economy rate is 40 ÷ 8 = 5.00 runs per over.

Importance of Economy Rate

Economy rate shows how effective a bowler is at restricting runs. While bowling average reflects wickets taken, economy rate reflects control and discipline. It is particularly important in ODIs and T20s, where limiting runs can often win matches.

Economy Rate in Different Formats

  • Test Cricket: Economy rate is less emphasized, but keeping it under 3 is considered excellent.
  • ODIs: An economy rate below 5 is good, while below 4.5 is excellent for frontline bowlers.
  • T20s: An economy rate below 7 is excellent, with 6 or less being elite in franchise leagues like IPL.

Economy Rate vs Bowling Average

Economy rate measures runs conceded per over, while bowling average measures runs conceded per wicket. Together, they provide a complete view of a bowler’s effectiveness. A bowler with a low average but high economy might be expensive but a wicket-taker, while one with a low economy but high average might be restrictive but less attacking.

Notable Economy Rate Records

  • Muttiah Muralitharan: Maintained superb economy in Tests and ODIs, below 3.5 in many formats.
  • Glenn McGrath: Known for economy rates around 3 in ODIs.
  • Rashid Khan: Modern-day T20 bowler with economy consistently under 6.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

1. What is a good economy rate in T20 cricket?

An economy rate below 7 is excellent, while 6 or less is considered elite.

2. Who has the best economy rate in ODIs?

Joel Garner and Glenn McGrath are known for their exceptional economy rates under 4 runs per over.

3. Does economy rate matter in Test cricket?

It matters less than in limited overs, but a low economy builds pressure and creates wicket-taking opportunities.

4. How is economy rate different from bowling average?

Economy rate measures runs per over, while bowling average measures runs per wicket.

5. Can a bowler with a high economy still be effective?

Yes, wicket-taking bowlers might concede more runs but still change the game with crucial breakthroughs.
