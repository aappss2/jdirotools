What is a SIP?

A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) lets you invest a fixed amount at regular intervals (usually monthly) into mutual funds — most often equity mutual funds. SIPs bring the benefits of rupee-cost averaging and disciplined investing. Instead of timing the market, a SIP invests consistently, which can reduce the impact of volatility over the long term.

How the SIP Calculator Works

The calculator assumes a fixed monthly contribution P, an expected annual rate R (in %), a compounding frequency (m times per year), and tenure in years T. The periodic rate used is r = R / 100 / m, and the total number of periods is n = m × T. For monthly SIP with monthly compounding, m = 12. The future value (FV) for regular end-of-period payments (ordinary annuity) is:

FV = P × [ ( (1 + r)^n - 1 ) / r ]

If payments are made at the period start (annuity due), multiply the result by (1 + r).

Example

If you invest ₹5,000 per month for 10 years at an expected annual return of 12% (monthly compounding):

  • P = 5,000
  • R = 12% → r = 0.12 / 12 = 0.01
  • n = 12 × 10 = 120

FV = 5,000 × [ (1.01^120 - 1) / 0.01 ] ≈ ₹12,58,682 (approx). Total invested = ₹5,000 × 120 = ₹6,00,000. Returns ≈ ₹6,58,682.

Why SIPs Work

  • Rupee-cost averaging: You buy more units when prices fall and fewer when prices rise.
  • Discipline: Regular investing helps build long-term wealth.
  • Power of compounding: Reinvested gains grow exponentially over time.

Tips for SIP Investors

  1. Start early to benefit from compounding.
  2. Increase SIP amount periodically (e.g., annually) to beat inflation.
  3. Choose a long-term horizon for equity SIPs (5–10+ years).
  4. Diversify across fund categories and risk levels.

FAQs

Q: Should I stop SIP during a market crash?

A: No — downturns are when SIPs benefit most due to rupee-cost averaging, unless your personal financial situation forces you to stop.

Q: Is SIP better than lump-sum?

A: SIP reduces timing risk and is suitable for investors who prefer disciplined investments. Lump-sum can outperform SIP if markets rally after investment, but SIP reduces downside risk.

Q: How realistic is the expected return?

A: Expected returns are estimates. Use conservative assumptions (8–12% for equity SIPs historically in India over long horizons) and review periodically.
