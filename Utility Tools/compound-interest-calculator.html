Understanding Compound Interest

Compound interest is the interest calculated on both the principal and the accumulated interest from previous periods. Unlike simple interest, which is calculated only on the principal amount, compound interest allows investments to grow faster because interest earns interest over time. Understanding and using compound interest is key to long-term financial planning, savings, and investments. This calculator demonstrates how your principal, interest rate, time, and contributions affect your wealth growth.

How Compound Interest Works

The formula for compound interest is A = P (1 + r/n)^(n*t) + contribution, where A is the total amount, P is principal, r is the annual interest rate, n is the number of compounding periods per year, t is the number of years, and contribution is any regular addition. Regular contributions and frequent compounding dramatically increase your total returns.

FAQ

1. What is the difference between simple and compound interest?

Simple interest is calculated only on the principal. Compound interest is calculated on principal + accumulated interest, resulting in faster growth.

2. How does compounding frequency affect growth?

The more frequently interest is compounded (daily > monthly > quarterly > annually), the faster your balance grows.

3. Can I include additional contributions?

Yes, regular contributions can be added each period to maximize growth over time.
