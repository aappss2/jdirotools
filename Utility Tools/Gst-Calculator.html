GST in India — Quick Guide

Goods and Services Tax (GST) is a comprehensive destination-based indirect tax levied on the supply of goods and services in India. It subsumed multiple indirect taxes like excise, VAT, service tax, and simplifies taxation by creating a single tax framework. GST rates in India include several slabs — 0%, 5%, 12%, 18%, and 28% — with certain goods attracting cess. This simple calculator helps you add or remove GST and create a printable invoice for transactions between businesses and consumers.

How GST is applied

For intra-state transactions (within the same state), GST is split equally between Central GST (CGST) and State GST (SGST). For inter-state transactions (between states), Integrated GST (IGST) applies and is collected by the central government. Example: At 18% GST, intra-state split = CGST 9% + SGST 9%; inter-state = IGST 18%.

Formulae & examples

To add GST (Net → Gross):
GST = Net × (rate / 100); Gross = Net + GST

To remove GST (Gross → Net):
Net = Gross / (1 + rate / 100); GST = Gross - Net

Example — Add GST: Net price = ₹1,000; GST rate = 18% → GST = ₹180; Gross = ₹1,180.

Example — Remove GST: Gross price = ₹1,180; GST rate = 18% → Net = 1180 / 1.18 = ₹1,000; GST = ₹180.

Common GST questions

1. Do I always charge GST?

If your business turnover exceeds the threshold notified by the government (with some variations by category), you must register and charge GST. Small suppliers may be exempt under composition schemes.

2. What is HSN / SAC?

HSN (Harmonized System of Nomenclature) codes classify goods; SAC (Services Accounting Code) does the same for services. These codes appear on GST invoices for compliance.

3. Does GST apply to exports?

Exports are generally zero-rated under GST (0%), meaning GST is not charged on export but input tax credit can be claimed or refunded subject to conditions.
